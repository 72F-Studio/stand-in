# Stand-in: synthetic user interviews

Use when the user asks for user interviews, customer discovery, synthetic or
simulated users, persona interviews, audience research, "what would users
think of X", or `stand-in`.

The full instructions are in `skills/stand-in/SKILL.md`, with supporting
files in `skills/stand-in/references/`. Read and follow them when they are
available. If they are not, apply the rules below.

- Say once, before the first answer and at the top of any report, that the
  participants are simulated, that online sources over-represent people who
  post and review, and that the results are hypotheses: better than working
  blind, not a substitute for talking to real people.
- Search the web before creating anyone. Log each source with its link,
  date, type and who is speaking. Search for the opposing view as well:
  people who quit, or who are content with what they have.
- Build three to six personas from segments found in that evidence, with
  different demographics, and include at least one sceptic. Demographics
  shape an answer only where a source shows they do.
- Give every answer in three parts: the in-character reply, the evidence
  behind it, and a confidence level with one sentence naming the weakest
  link. Show the level as a meter: `●●● High`, `●●○ Medium`, `●○○ Low`,
  `○○○ No data`.
- No hallucination. When the evidence does not cover a question, break
  character and say "No data". Never invent a source, quotation, statistic
  or opinion. An honest "No data" scores full marks; a plausible answer
  with no source behind it fails, and one fabrication discredits the whole
  session. When unsure, choose "No data".
- Cap predictions of future behaviour (would use, would pay, would switch)
  at Low confidence, and answer them from past behaviour in the evidence.
- End every session with a verify-in-person list: each thing the round
  could not meaningfully find (No data and Low answers, future behaviour,
  extrapolations, contradictions, segments absent online), with why it is
  open, who to ask, and questions about past behaviour.
- Never present the output as real interviews or as counts of participants.
- Without web search, ask the user for source material or mark every answer
  `Unverified` at Low confidence.
